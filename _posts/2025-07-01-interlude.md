The blasted wastelands of Alberetor stretched before the travelers like a wound
upon the earth, a desolate expanse where life had been scoured away by relentless
corruption. The sky hung low, choked with ash-gray clouds that seemed to
suffocate the sun, casting the world in a perpetual twilight. The ground was a
mosaic of cracked, barren rock and swirling dust, the soil long since stripped
by merciless winds. No birds sang, no insects buzzed—only the mournful howl of
the wind broke the silence, carrying with it the acrid tang of decay. Cities
and towns, once bustling with life, now stood as hollow husks, their stone walls
eerily preserved in the absence of rot, frozen in time like tombstones in a
forgotten graveyard. For over a week, the party — Agathara, Kurano, Reginald,
and Lamerra — trudged through this forsaken land, their wagon creaking under
the strain, pulled by mules that grew weaker with each passing day. Little Bear,
their wolf-bear-dog companion, now the size of a large pony, loped beside them,
his once- plump frame honed lean and muscular by the endless march.

###Day One: The Weight of Desolation

The journey began with a grim dawn, the horizon barely discernible through the
haze of blowing dust. Agathara, her white cleric’s robes already streaked with
grime, led the group with a resolute stride, her faith in Prios a flickering
light against the oppressive darkness. “This land is cursed,” she muttered,
clutching her holy symbol as she scanned the barren expanse. “Prios weeps for
it.”

Kurano, his iron-sworn armor dulled by dust, kept his hand on his blade, eyes
darting to every shadow. “Cursed or not, something’s watching us. I can feel it.”
His hunter’s instincts, honed by years of tracking monsters, prickled with unease,
though no foe emerged from the desolation.

Reginald, ever the scholar, scribbled in his journal, his spectacles fogged with
grit. “Fascinating,” he murmured, brushing away dust to examine a patch of
exposed rock. “The corruption has stripped even the microbial life. This is no
mere desert—it’s a graveyard of existence itself.”

Lamerra, the dwarven necromancer, trailed behind, his dark cloak blending with
the ashen landscape. His undead sentries, kept at a distance to avoid Agathara’s
wrath, scouted the periphery. “Life’s gone, but death lingers,” he said, his
voice low. “This place is ripe for my kind of work.” Agathara shot him a glare
but said nothing, her focus on the path ahead.

The wagon, laden with their dwindling supplies, groaned as the mules strained
against the rocky terrain. By midday, they found a small, brackish pool
nestled in a hollow, its surface shimmering with an oily sheen. Agathara knelt,
her hands glowing with Prios’s light as she purified the water. It was potable,
but the taste was bitter, metallic, like licking rusted iron. “It’ll keep us
alive,” she said, grimacing as she filled their skins. Little Bear lapped at it
cautiously, his massive form casting a long shadow.

###Day Three: The Fallen Mule

The days blurred into a relentless grind of dust and heat. The mules, already
weakened, struggled to pull the wagon over jagged outcrops of stone. On the
third day, one of the beasts — a wiry creature they’d nicknamed Stubborn —
faltered, its legs buckling under the weight of exhaustion. Kurano tried to coax
it forward, but it collapsed with a pitiful bray, its eyes dull with defeat.

“We can’t save it,” Kurano said, his voice heavy as he drew his dagger. The act
was swift, merciful, but the loss hit hard. The remaining mules snorted nervously,
and Little Bear growled, sensing the group’s unease. They redistributed the
wagon’s load, lightening it as best they could, but the absence of Stubborn
weighed on their spirits.

Lamerra knelt beside the fallen mule, murmuring softly. “I could raise it,” he
offered, his eyes glinting with necromantic intent. Agathara’s response was
immediate, her voice sharp as a blade. “You will not defile it. Let it rest.”
Lamerra shrugged, but his sentries lingered closer that night, their unseen
presence a silent provocation.

###Day Five: The Dust Storm

By the fifth day, the group reached the skeletal remains of a town, its stone
buildings standing like ghosts in the haze. The wind had grown fiercer,
whipping the air into a frenzy of stinging grit.  As they explored the empty
streets, seeking shelter, the sky darkened, and a low, ominous roar rolled across
the wastes.

“Dust storm!” Kurano shouted, grabbing the reins of the mules. They scrambled
into the husk of a tavern, its roof half-collapsed but sturdy enough to shield
them. Little Bear wedged himself against Agathara, his massive bulk a comforting
weight as the storm descended. The wind screamed, tearing at the town with claws
of dust and debris. Outside, the world vanished in a churning maelstrom, the
ground itself seeming to writhe as the storm scoured away what little remained of
Alberetor’s vitality.

Inside, the group huddled, their faces wrapped in cloths to filter the invasive
dust. Reginald coughed, clutching his journal protectively. “This is worse than
any storm I’ve read about,” he wheezed. “It’s as if the land itself is trying
to erase us.”

Lamerra, his voice muffled, chuckled darkly. “The land’s dead, but it’s still
angry.” Agathara prayed softly, her words a faint counterpoint to the storm’s
fury, beseeching Prios for protection.  Hours passed before the wind relented,
leaving the town buried in drifts of gray dust, the air thick with the taste of
ash.

###Day Seven: The Acid Rain

The seventh day brought a new torment. The clouds, heavy with unnatural weight,
unleashed a deluge of rain that burned as it fell. Lightning cracked incessantly,
illuminating the wasteland in stark, blinding flashes. The rain sizzled on the
ground, pitting the exposed rock and leaving faint, acrid fumes. The group donned
their cloaks, pulling the wagon beneath a crumbling stone archway for shelter.
Little Bear whined, his fur singed where stray droplets struck.

Agathara worked tirelessly, her divine magic straining to purify the meager
water they collected from a nearby gully. “This rain is poison,” she said, her
face pale with exertion. “The corruption runs deep here.” The purified water
still carried a sharp, chemical bite, but it was all they had.  Kurano stood
watch, his armor pitted by the acidic downpour. “We’re not alone,” he warned,
pointing to faint tracks in the mud—humanoid, but twisted, as if made by something
not entirely alive. Lamerra’s eyes narrowed. “Undead, maybe. Or worse.” His
sentries reported nothing, but the group’s nerves were frayed, the constant
threat of the unknown wearing them down.  Reginald, soaked and shivering, tried
to lighten the mood. “At least Little Bear’s thriving,” he said, patting the
beast’s muscular flank. The wolf-bear-dog, now a formidable presence, nuzzled
him back, his loyalty a rare spark of warmth in the bleakness.

###Day Nine: The Final Push

By the ninth day, the group was gaunt, their supplies nearly gone. The remaining
mules staggered, their ribs stark against their hides. Little Bear, though
leaner, remained vigilant, his golden eyes scanning the horizon. The wasteland
seemed endless, a sea of barren rock and swirling dust, but faint signs of their
destination appeared—old wagon tracks, half-buried, leading toward a distant
valley.

Agathara’s prayers grew quieter, her faith tested by the relentless desolation.
Kurano’s vigilance never wavered, but his shoulders slumped under the weight of
constant alertness. Reginald’s journal was nearly full, its pages a chronicle of
a land forsaken. Lamerra, ever stoic, kept his thoughts to himself, though his
sentries’ presence was a constant reminder of the group’s uneasy alliance.

As they crested a final ridge late in the evening, the valley of Convent School
of Luminous Laws came into view, its stone walls a defiant bastion against the
corruption. But the journey had taken its toll. The deadlands of Alberetor had
tested their endurance, their resolve, and their unity. They were alive, but the
darkness of the land clung to them, a shadow that would not easily fade.
